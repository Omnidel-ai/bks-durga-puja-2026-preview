Cultural context

VERIFIED In December 2021, ‘Durga Puja in Kolkata’ was inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The inscribed element is Kolkata’s Puja — not this page, and not an unnamed venue.

RESEARCH-DERIVED UNESCO describes unfired Ganga clay, eyes painted from Mahalaya, immersion on the tenth day, and homecoming. Traditional Bengali drumming and urban pavilions are part of that public performance of religion and art.

RESEARCH-DERIVED Public community Pujas are often called barowari or sarbojanin — for a neighbourhood, not only a household. BKS will not use those words as its committee type until a committee exists and chooses them.

RESEARCH-DERIVED Bhog is food offered and then shared. Community kitchens often serve khichuri, mixed vegetables, fries, chutney and payesh. Whether BKS will serve bhog is not decided here.

The public days — cultural sequence, not a BKS timetable

Meanings below are research-derived. Gregorian clocks for 2026 are not printed as ritual fact.

  • RESEARCH-DERIVED

    Mahalaya

    Pitru Paksha ends. In Bengal this dawn is also cultural memory: the goddess is turned toward the living year.

  • RESEARCH-DERIVED

    Shashthi

    Bodhon — the public awakening of the image.

  • RESEARCH-DERIVED

    Saptami

    Nabapatrika / Kolabou. Full public worship typically begins.

  • RESEARCH-DERIVED

    Ashtami

    Pushpanjali, Kumari Puja, and Sandhi Puja at the join of Ashtami and Navami.

  • RESEARCH-DERIVED

    Navami

    Culminating worship in many committees.

  • RESEARCH-DERIVED

    Dashami

    Sindoor Khela, Bijoya, and immersion — often on a civil day also fixed with the police.
